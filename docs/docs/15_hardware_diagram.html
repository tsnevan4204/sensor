Wall Sensor Hardware

Every physical part of the two-band prototype, how the signal moves through it, and which term in the inversion each part feeds. The 60–64 GHz radar chip and capture board are chosen; the UWB front end, lens, IMU and host are specified by requirement but not yet sourced.

One measurement, in order

  1. Factory, once: scan the metal plate at staggered distances; store the antenna tables in flash.
  2. Hold the sensor about 30 cm from the wall. Still is best; slow motion is fitted as extra frames.
  3. Sweep both bands: 256 UWB steps and a burst of 60–64 GHz chirps. Averaging 100 chirps takes about 2 ms and adds 20 dB.
  4. Capture the reference (leakage and coupler tap) in the same burst.
  5. Send raw samples to the host and apply the factory tables.
  6. Invert: fit drift, standoff, layer count, thicknesses and materials together; raise the χ² alarm if nothing fits.
  7. Report each layer: material class, thickness and error bar.

Design rules to carry into the schematic

Put the RF and PLL rails on their own low-noise LDOs; the inversion fits phase, so supply ripple on the synthesizer becomes phase error.

Route one clock to both radios. Two free-running oscillators would add an unknown phase per band.

Keep the antennas, lens and radome fixed relative to each other: anything that moves after factory calibration becomes drift the fit has to absorb, at the cost of wider error bars (about 4.6× on εr in E035).

Choose a UWB front end that exposes raw complex samples per frequency step. An impulse radar that only reports ranges will not work with this algorithm.
